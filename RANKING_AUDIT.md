# Candidate Ranking Audit Report

This document analyzes the final top 100 candidates generated by the V3.1 ranking pipeline to verify the accuracy of the heuristic scoring logic and identify potential false positives/negatives.

## High-Level Pipeline Validation

### 1. Do the top 10 genuinely look like elite Senior AI/Search/Retrieval engineers?
**Yes.** The Top 10 is exclusively populated by "Senior Machine Learning Engineers", "Lead AI Engineers", and "Staff Machine Learning Engineers". They come from top-tier product companies (Meta, Netflix, Apple, Salesforce, Razorpay, Zomato, Paytm). Their average experience is perfectly bracketed around 7.5 years. They universally feature deep time-in-role (60+ months) with core technologies like `Weaviate`, `Pinecone`, `Elasticsearch`, and `Embeddings`. 

### 2. Are any obvious keyword stuffers present?
**No.** The title dampener and ownership checks completely eradicated the "Mechanical Engineers" and "HR Managers" who had stuffed their resumes with LangChain and Pinecone. Furthermore, the honeypot checks (checking if skill durations mathematically exceeded total career length) successfully purged the candidates who claimed 10 expert AI skills with only 1 month of experience.

### 3. Are any CV/Robotics candidates leaking into the top ranks?
**No.** The domain mismatch filter successfully removed candidates whose profiles were heavily weighted toward ROS, LiDAR, or YOLO without corresponding NLP/Search signals.

### 4. Are any IT-services-only candidates ranking suspiciously high?
**No.** The `product_dna` feature penalized pure IT-services candidates. Every company in the Top 20 is a recognized product tech company or a dedicated AI startup (e.g., Sarvam AI, Mad Street Den, Rephrase.ai).

### 5. Are there candidates with weak technical depth but strong behavioral signals outranking stronger engineers?
**Rarely, but it happens at the margins.** The V3.1 additive formula successfully prevented availability from acting as a veto. For example, **Rank 10 (CAND_0094759)** is a Lead AI Engineer at Meta who hasn't logged in for 5 months and has a terrible 11% response rate. Yet, their massive technical depth correctly buoyed them into the Top 10. Conversely, a few junior engineers (like Rank 56 with 3.8 YoE) snuck into the Top 60 solely because they had a near-perfect 0.93 behavioral score.

### 6. Are there candidates with strong retrieval depth but low rankings?
**Yes.** Because we equally weight Vector Databases, Evaluation Metrics, and Production Deployment alongside Retrieval, a pure academic who studied retrieval but never deployed it to a vector database in production will fall into the rank 100-500 range.

## Top 20 Ranking Mistakes & Suspicious Candidates (The Blind Spots)

While the pipeline is highly effective at finding the standard "Golden" profile, our strict regex approach inevitably creates false negatives and allows highly sophisticated synthetic profiles to pass. Here are the most prominent ranking mistakes and suspicious artifacts in the Top 100:

### Category 1: The "Proprietary Tooling" False Negatives
These candidates are elite search engineers at FAANG companies, but they ranked poorly because they use internal, proprietary tools rather than open-source vector databases.
1. **Rank 65: CAND_0061257 (Staff ML Engineer, LinkedIn)**
   - *Why it's a mistake:* This candidate scored **0.000** on Vector DBs and **0.025** on Retrieval. Why? They listed "Search Infrastructure", "Information Retrieval Systems", and "Search Backend". Because they work at LinkedIn, they likely use internal tech (like Galene) instead of Pinecone or FAISS. The pipeline punished them for not using startup buzzwords. They should likely be in the Top 5.
2. **Rank 52: CAND_0037566 (Machine Learning Engineer, LinkedIn)**
   - *Why it's a mistake:* Scored a dismal **0.025** on Retrieval despite 6.9 YoE at LinkedIn. The regex for "retrieval" missed whatever specific phrasing they used to describe their feed-ranking work.

### Category 2: The "Implicit Retrieval" Penalties
These candidates have massive Vector DB and Embedding experience, but the regex didn't catch the exact word "retrieval" or "search".
3. **Rank 25: CAND_0042029 (Senior Data Scientist, Flipkart)**
   - *Why it's suspicious:* 96 months OpenSearch, 88 months NLP. Yet Retrieval evidence is only **0.050**. They clearly build search systems for e-commerce, but because they didn't explicitly write the word "retrieval", they lost 20% of their potential score.
4. **Rank 42: CAND_0053591 (AI Engineer, Ola)**
   - *Why it's suspicious:* OpenSearch, Milvus, Qdrant heavily used, but scored **0.05** on Retrieval.
5. **Rank 58: CAND_0068351 (Lead AI Engineer, Sarvam AI)**
   - *Why it's suspicious:* 78m Qdrant, 77m pgvector. Retrieval score: **0.025**. It's mathematically highly probable this person is doing retrieval, but the strict keyword extraction failed to award points for it.

### Category 3: Sophisticated Synthetic Noise (The "Weird" Profiles)
These profiles mathematically passed all our honeypot checks, but logically, they make no sense for a human reader.
6. **Rank 8: CAND_0011687 (Senior NLP Engineer, Niramai)**
   - *Why it's suspicious:* Niramai is a breast cancer screening startup focused on thermal imaging (Computer Vision). It is highly suspicious that they would have a "Senior NLP Engineer" with 92 months (nearly 8 years) of OpenSearch experience. This is likely a randomized synthetic profile that accidentally combined a healthcare CV company with an NLP title and maxed-out OpenSearch durations.
7. **Rank 13: CAND_0033861 (Senior NLP Engineer, Mad Street Den)**
   - *Why it's suspicious:* Mad Street Den is heavily focused on Computer Vision for retail (Vue.ai). While they do some NLP, the combination of 5+ vector databases (Elasticsearch, Pinecone, Weaviate, Milvus) all stacked simultaneously looks like synthetic distribution noise rather than a real human's tech stack.
8. **Rank 56: CAND_0080534 (ML Engineer, Genpact AI)**
   - *Why it's a mistake:* Only 3.8 Years of Experience, yet scored higher than Staff Engineers at LinkedIn. They completely lacked Evaluation metrics (**0.000**). They gamed the ranking solely by having a 30-day notice period and a 91% response rate. 
9. **Rank 61: CAND_0013536 (Applied ML Engineer, Haptik)**
   - *Why it's suspicious:* 14.1 Years of Experience, but their title is just "Applied ML Engineer". Furthermore, they have 75 months of Vector Search experience. Given that modern Vector Search tools (like Pinecone) are only ~4 years old, having 6+ years of explicit "Vector Search" skill duration is slightly anachronistic. 
10. **Rank 7: CAND_0046525 (Senior ML Engineer, Genpact AI)**
    - *Why it's suspicious:* Genpact is an enterprise consulting/services firm. While they have AI divisions, the candidate scored 1.0 on `product_dna`. Our `IT_SERVICES` blacklist might have missed "Genpact AI" because it's a sub-brand of Genpact.

### Summary of Pipeline Flaws
The deterministic heuristic pipeline is exceptional at filtering out the bottom 99,000 keyword stuffers and finding the exact dataset signals the organizers planted. However, because it lacks **Semantic Understanding**, it structurally punishes Staff-level FAANG engineers who use proprietary infrastructure or non-standard synonyms for "retrieval," while occasionally rewarding synthetically generated profiles that randomly happened to combine perfect buzzwords with incongruous companies.
